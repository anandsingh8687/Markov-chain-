# v13 candidate: sell the late harvests at the morning peak (MDX)

Status: **submitted 2026-09-26 as Kaggle submission 56581155** (`main.py`, repo 6fe8665). Previously live:
v12 (submission 56575662).

v13 is v12 plus one layer, MDX, added in the entrypoint after RDX. From day 18,
at 5am and 5pm, it sells the whole shed stock of strawberries, milk and wool
while the quote is at least $20. The sale goes first in the market queue. The
layer is in `research/v13/mdx_layer.py`; `research/v13/build_mdx.py` splices it
into a base.

```python
MDX_ITEMS = ("STRAWBERRY", "MILK", "WOOL")
MDX_FROM_DAY = 18
MDX_HOURS = (5, 17)
MDX_MINP = 20
```

## 1. Where v12 loses

v12 wins 75 of v11's 98 ladder replays. 17 of its 23 losses are by less than
$1,000. `research/tools/v10/lossdiag12.py` splits each loss by product. The
recurring item was strawberries: both sides sold the same ~249 berries, but
the opponent averaged $2-5 more a berry. On 249 berries that is $500-1,250,
the size of the close losses.

A sale timeline (`research/tools/v10/salelog.py`) of one of these losses (Ian
lin, 113521400) shows the cause. `research/v13/sprobe.py` then gives our shed
stock and orders by hour. Both sides run the same route tape, so the sales
match to the unit until day 22. After that:

* The tape's late strawberry harvests arrive in the shed at midnight, about 21
  berries on day 26.
* v12 held them through the morning while the quote rose from $49 to $66. Then
  the lead-sellers trickled them out 2-3 an hour from hour 8.
* The opponent sold its batch at hour 5 or 9, at the top of the curve. We sold
  into the crash it left, at $26 down to $3.

Only the rival's arrival changes a quote within a day: a shop drains 6 units a
day. So trickling a batch out never helps, and against a rival holding the
same batch, the first seller takes the top of the curve. RDX (v12) did not
catch this. It caps its sale at what the tape plans for the next 16 turns,
which here was 2 berries.

### Why the lineage stalls below the top 100

Leaderboard on 2026-09-26 13:28:
* #1 3087, #10 2893, #50 2736, **#100 2643**, #200 2537, #300 2485;
* v12 2446 (rank 391, still settling), v11 2363, v10 2380.

Rank 100 has fallen from 2786 to 2643 in four days, and all our submissions
fall with the pool.

* Up to about 2650 the ladder is mostly copies of the same public route tape.
  Games are decided by a few hundred dollars of selling order. That is where
  v11, v12 and v13 gain.
* In v10's 177 ladder games, teams at 2600+ beat our lineage 13 games to 1.
  In the replayed top-team games, 2800+ teams bank a median $107.6k, against
  v12's $96.5k on the ladder. Those teams run different, larger economies,
  and no selling layer closes an $11k gap.
* An Elo-style reading of the table above puts v13's level around 2550-2600:
  it wins about 80% against 2400-2500 and 43% against 2500-2600. That is
  about rank 150-250. Top 100 needs about 50% against 2600-2700 opponents.
