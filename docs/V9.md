# v9: fix the cash crunch that lost v8's two biggest games

v9 is v8 plus two changes:

* the **morning-hire reserve** (new), which fixes v8's collapse losses;
* the **cheaper opening** from Herd Safe v3 (buy 8 / sell 3 wheat instead of buy 20 / sell 15).

The last sections compare v9 with the current top 10 and with the newest public agents.

## v8 on the ladder

v8 (submission 56503612) went live on 2026-09-23. Over its first 94 ladder games
it went **79-15 (84%)** with no ties, and was rated **2188.9** on 2026-09-24 while
still climbing. Over the same window v7 went 9-6-25.

All 15 losses were downloaded and replayed. The local engine reproduces every
one of them to the dollar. They fall into two groups:

| Group | Games | Size | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Close | 13 | −$20 to −$1,858 | The opponent runs a near-clone of the same route and wins on sale timing, or grows carrot where we grow wheat |
| Collapse | 2 | **−$28,063**, −$10,813 | See below |

## The collapse

Both big losses follow the same chain:

1. **Turn 0.** The opening's wheat buy-and-sell runs in per-unit lockstep against
   a rival with a slightly different opening. It comes out a few dollars dearer
   for us than usual.
2. **End of day 0.** The tape spends the day's cash down to the dollar, so we end
   the day with **$1**. The rival ends it with $22.
3. **Day 1, hour 0.** Hands are wiped every night and re-hired, and the n-th hire
   of a day costs fib(n): $1, $1, $2, $3, and so on. The tape hires 3 hands for $4.
   We can afford 1.
4. **Day 2.** Two hands short for a day means less watering and feeding, which
   means less income, so the day-2 cow purchase fails.
5. **The rest of the game.** Every later animal purchase is a turn late or fails.
   We finish with 5 sheep and 5 cows against the rival's 9 and 6, and half its
   wool.

The replay confirms it. Across the 45 v8 ladder games checked, a hire failed in
exactly two, and those are these two losses.

## The fix

The **morning-hire reserve** is appended to `main.py`. From hour 12 of each day it
reads how many hands the tape hires at the next hour 0 and computes their cost.
If this turn's orders would leave less than that, it trims seed purchases,
starting from the end of the list, until the cost is covered. Seeds are the
cheapest thing on the list to lose: one missing wheat seed costs one plant,
while two missing hands cost a day of the whole schedule.

The guard does nothing unless cash would actually run short. In the two collapse
games it removed a single $10 wheat seed:

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 112629718 | Jeryos | −$28,063 | **+$17,017** |
| 112667026 | Tashfik Salehin | −$10,813 | **+$22,356** |

## Tried for v9 and rejected

Each was measured head-to-head against v8 on 16 fresh seeds, and on v8's
downloaded ladder games:

* **Carrot swap at a lower price ratio** (1.5 instead of 1.8, and with the last
  day raised from 23 to 25): 0 W, 13 T, 3 L against v8. Close losses to carrot
  growers are not fixed by growing more carrot.
* **Race horizon 46:** 5-6-5 against v8 (−$34).
* **Race horizon 42:** 1-2-13 against v8.
* **Counter D budget 2,000 instead of 800:** 3-13-0 (+$3). Too small to be worth
  the extra time per turn.
* **Alternative routes for yarn-shop layouts:** the table's route 9 won 76 of 90
  ghost games (+$2,716). Route 0 won 57 (+$906), and routes 11, 12 and 128 were
  far worse.

## What the top 10 do differently

The top-10 teams' current submissions were found by crawling episodes, starting
from v8's opponents. 80 of their recent games were downloaded (8 per team), and
their production plans were compared with v8's own games:

| | Top 10 (range) | v8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| land plots bought | **2-4** (6 of 10 average 3+), by day 7-8 | 2.2, around day 9 |
| cows | **7-14** (9 of 10 at 8 or more) | 7.4 |
| melon seeds | **12-25** (half at 21+) | 12 |
| tomato seeds | **0-21** (7 of 10 grow some) | ~1 |
| hires per day | **8.9-11.3** | 9.1 |
| first turn | a cow and 5 wheat (5 of the top 7) | a wheat round trip |

On real ladder games, the top teams face opponents about as strong as v8's but
finish $15-20k further ahead:

| | Mean bank | Opponents' mean | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v8, 104 games | $96.9k | $91.6k | +$5.3k |
| DSM (#1), 129 games; 126-1 in its last 127 | $112.1k | $89.7k | +$22.4k |
| M & M & P & Q (#3) | $116.0k | $92.1k | +$23.9k |
| Unknown Mother-Goose (#5) | $113.6k | $94.2k | +$19.4k |

Their edge is **scale**: they reinvest everything early, buying the third plot
of land, a bigger herd and twice the melon. A public analysis of the ladder
("What 2600+ Farms Do Differently", Georgy Mamarin) describes the same shape:
the bank stays near zero deep into the season, then compounds. None of the
public agents tested opens this way, so this lineage is private.

**What this means for us.** The base v9 is built on replays fixed 719-turn
tapes. Those tapes use two plots and a fixed herd. Adopting the top teams' plan
means new tapes, or a planner that can use the third plot, and that is a v10
project, not a parameter change.

**A warning about the ghost benchmark at the top.** Replaying top-team tapes
against v9 says v9 wins 60 of 68, often by $60k or more. That is not real. Top
agents spend down to zero, so once v9 changes the prices they face, their
recorded purchases fail and their whole game collapses. Against reinvesting
agents, only closed-loop play and real ladder banks are evidence.

## The newest public agents

Eleven public agents published on 2026-09-22 and 23 were each tested against v9
on 8 seeds. v9 beat most of them 6-8 of 8. Four of them were stronger:

| Agent | v9's record |
| --- | --- |
| Herd Safe v3 (arsgorynich), and The Shepherd's Ledger, a copy of it | 2-6 |
| V40 Challenger | 3-5 |
| Pioneers of Kaggle Town, candidate 2 | 3-5 |

Re-measured on 16 more seeds, v9 went 7-9 against Herd Safe v3, 13-3 against V40
Challenger and 12-4 against Pioneers. Herd Safe v3 is tetsutani's stack with
five more layers: the cheaper opening, fertilizer skipping, a late seed cap,
feed safety and a longer sales forecast.

The obvious move is to rebase on it, so that was tested: Herd Safe v3 plus v9's
level-2 counter D and hire guard (`hs_l2hr`), on the same 16 seeds.

| Opponent | v9 | Herd Safe + v9 layers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| prv_rain | **13-3** | 7-9 |
| tetsutani | 16-0 | 16-0 |
| V40 Challenger | 13-3 | 12-4 |
| Pioneers | 12-4 | 12-4 |
| 2965 Engine | 15-1 | 16-0 |
| Herd Safe v3 | 7-9 | **11-5-0** |
| head to head | 7-9, but +$58 a game | 9-7 |

Overall the two are equal: about 0.79-0.80 against this field. So v9 keeps its
base and takes only Herd Safe's opening, which is a strict gain. Which base is
better on the ladder depends on how fast the Herd Safe lineage spreads. Re-check
the ladder's losses in a few days.
