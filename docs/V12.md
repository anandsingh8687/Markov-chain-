# v12: opponent-aware sale timing

v12 (`candidates/v12/main.py`) is v11 plus two changes.

1. **Late selling retuned against today's ladder.** The base's lead-sellers look
   16 turns ahead of the route tape instead of 12, from day 15 instead of day 18.
   Strawberries are now included, and the midday window starts at hour 8.
2. **RDX, rival dump anticipation (new).** Each turn the base recovers the
   rival's sales from the market's inventory change, the shops' fixed draw and
   our own sells. RDX learns, per product, the hours at which this rival has
   sold 6 or more units at once. Once it has seen that on **2 separate days**,
   it sells, one turn before the next predicted dump, what our tape would have
   sold of that product in the next 16 turns, first in our order queue. Against
   a rival that does not dump, it never fires.

## Why

In the 24 hours to 2026-09-26, v11 went 14-16 in its last 30 games, and our
rank fell from 332 to 532. 26 of its 30 losses were close races (−$12 to
−$1,401) against copies of our own lineage selling the same volumes at $3-5
more per strawberry and wool unit. One replay showed the pattern: on days 26
and 28, at hour 12, the rival sells its whole batch of 18 strawberries at once,
first in its queue, while we sell 3 a turn into the crash.

## Which test bed to trust

Two test beds disagreed:

* **Replays of real ladder opponents.** Exact reproduction of the recorded
  games, but the opponent cannot react.
* **Live play against public agents** (Shepherd's Ledger, Master Engine V4,
  Four-Turn Forecast, Harvest Ledger). These react to our selling, and they
  punish selling early.

Live ladder data decided it. In the same period and against the same rating
bands, v11 (early selling) did much better than v10:

| Opponent score | v10 live | v11 live |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2400-2500 | 4-15 | **11-17** |
| 2300-2400 | 7-6 | **21-6** |
| 2500+ | 1-11 | 3-9 |

So the replays predict the ladder, and the reactive public agents are not
representative of it. v12 was optimised on the replays and confirmed on a
second, independent sample.

## Results

| Test bed | v10 | v11 | **v12** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| today's opponents (v11's 98 ladder games) | 59 | 68 | **75** (8 losses → wins, 1 win → loss) |
| yesterday's opponents (v10's 127 ladder games) | 77 | 89 | **97** |
| 412-game archive (v3-v8 era) | 379 | 383 | 382 |

Runtime: 1.7 ms a turn on average, 73 ms at worst. Both seats finish `DONE`.

Variants tried on the same 98 games (v11 = 68):

| Variant | Wins |
| --- | --- |
| v11m: look-ahead 16, midday window from hour 8 | 72 |
| + strawberries | 72 |
| + day 15 start | 73 |
| + strawberries + day 15 start | 74 |
| + whole-batch pull | 72 |
| + dawn window to hour 5 | 72 |
| RDX on v10 | 71, but 60 with strict settings |
| RDX on v11: (days, units) = (2, 10) / (2, 6) / (1, 10) | 69 / 71 / 71 |
| **strawberries + day 15 start + RDX (2, 6)** | **75** |

**Known cost:** against reactive public agents in live play, early selling
loses 1-3 more games in 24 than v10 does (for example Shepherd's Ledger 18/24
against 23/24). The live ladder results above say that population is not what
we face.
